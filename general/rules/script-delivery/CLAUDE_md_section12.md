
## 12. The user's own word first; scripts are executor work; ask only what nothing answers

Set by the user on 2026-11-27, after an executor asked for the correct script answered
with a page of lessons, three questions and "frozen ... until you answer", and the hold it
cited came from another session's correction notice; verbatim: "use exactly the format
now inside ~/AI-management. Why do you even ask?", and "your restriction to "only a
manager can ..." sucks. Executor is cheaper than manager and they should learn to give
out correct scripts". The rules below bind the seat, whatever the brand.

- A direct instruction from a session's own user outranks a hold, a plan decision, a
  handover or a notice from any other session: the session does it and tells its
  manager. Only what needs an approval of its own still waits for that approval (a
  gated push, a deletion, others' repositories, a budget beyond the plan).
- Building and delivering a correct script is executor work. No order, handover or
  approval from a manager gates it; the manager's part is the check. A planner never
  reserves a script's building or delivery to itself or to its handover.
- When the user asks for a deliverable, the reply is the deliverable with its check: no
  page of lessons, restated incident or list of decisions in its place.
- A question goes to the user only when the user's own words, the rules, the plan and
  the design of the work do not answer it; the format of a script is never a question
  (it is the templates', and only the code that fetches or reads is adapted).
- Every hold a planner writes names the user's direct instruction as what lifts it;
  ~/AI-management/general/tools/handover-lint/check_spec.py warns on one that does not
  (HOLD_WITHOUT_USER).
